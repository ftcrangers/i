The competition

What is FIRST Tech Challenge?

FTC brings students together to design, build, and program robots for a new game challenge each season. Teams compete in alliances while developing engineering, communication, and problem-solving skills.

More than a robot

Engineering, collaboration, and competition.

Teams work through the full engineering cycle: explore a challenge, design a solution, build and program it, then test and improve. Outreach and teamwork matter just as much as what happens on the field.

CAD & build

From first sketch to field-ready.

We use Onshape CAD and an iterative engineering process to make design decisions, test ideas, and improve reliability.

Find the problem

Define the challenge and identify what the robot needs to do consistently.

Brainstorm

Compare possible designs, look for trade-offs, and choose practical concepts.

Create a plan

Break the robot into drivetrain, intake, transfer, and outtake systems.

Design & build

Use CAD and hands-on prototyping to turn the plan into a working robot.

Test

Run match-like tests to find weak points and collect useful feedback.

Improve

Refine mechanisms, strengthen the design, and make performance more repeatable.

Inside the machine

Meet the Rangers robot.

Every mechanism has a job. Our robot combines a compact drivetrain, a purpose-built scoring system, and sensors that help it navigate the field.

Drivetrain · Swyft Drives

Our drivetrain uses Swyft Drives, selected for a compact setup, speed, and durability.

Scoring hood

A shaped hood helps guide game pieces toward a more consistent scoring trajectory. We refine its angle and geometry through repeated testing.

Limelight vision

The Limelight camera helps identify AprilTags for alignment and can also support game-piece color detection.

Odometry & pathing

Odometry pods provide movement measurements that support autonomous navigation and Pedro Pathing routines.

Programming

Code that brings the robot to life.
